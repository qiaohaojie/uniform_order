# Research: Australian second-hand school uniform trading

**Status:** research, plan-only (no product work)  
**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Scope:** how Australian schools, P&Cs/P&Fs, and parents actually donate, sell, buy, and trade preloved uniforms in 2024–2026.  
**Companion:** [plan-preloved-shop.md](./plan-preloved-shop.md)

This note records what already exists. It does not invent a marketplace model.

An independent verification pass (2026-09-06) reached the same shop-side picture. It also mixed in UK/NZ pages (Ratcliffe College, Uniformerly, Albany Junior High). Those are excluded here. Australian sources from that pass that were inspected and held up are folded in below.

---

## 1. Bottom line

Australian families already use **three parallel systems**. They are not the same product.

| System | Who sells | Who inspects | Where money goes | Typical fulfilment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **School / P&C preloved shop** | The shop (seller of record) | Shop volunteers | P&C (donation) or split with the family (consignment) | Pickup at the uniform shop |
| **Parent-to-parent classifieds** | The parent | The buyer, at handover | The parent | School gate / PayID / cash |
| **Dedicated C2C platforms** | The parent (or a P&C account on the platform) | Photos + buyer | The parent, minus any platform fee | Pickup or post |

UniformOrder is a **school uniform shop**. The widely accepted way for a shop like this is system 1: a P&C-run second-hand rack next to the new-uniform counter.

Parents drop washed current-uniform items at the shop. Volunteers inspect, price, and sell. Other parents buy from the shop and collect at the same pickup window as new stock. That is the model to copy. It is not a Facebook clone, not a nationwide marketplace, and not a barter ledger.

## 2. How families actually source preloved uniforms

A 2023 Nine.com.au parenting guide lists the same sequence most parents still use: ask the school first, then official and unofficial Facebook groups, then suburb Marketplace / Gumtree, then specialist websites, then cheaper new retailers for unbranded basics.[^nine]

That order matters. **The school shop is the first place parents are told to look.** Facebook is the overflow when the shop is closed, out of size, or does not exist.

Rethread’s 2026 consumer guide restates the same three channels: online marketplaces, school second-hand shops, and Facebook groups. School shops are described as typically open a few times per year (often at the start of term), cash or EFTPOS, stock limited to donations, priced low, and sold without returns or condition guarantees.[^rethread-buy]

School Locker and similar retailers sell **new** uniforms. They are not a second-hand channel.[^nine]

## 3. The school / P&C preloved shop (the common shop model)

This is the operating model UniformOrder should enable. It is documented, repeated, and already understood by P&C volunteers.

### 3.1 What it is

A second-hand counter (sometimes a separate “Preloved Uniforms” room) run by the P&C, P&F, or the school uniform shop. It sits on campus. It is a service to families and a fundraiser. Manly Campus P&C’s volunteer-run shop reports almost $10,000 a year with almost no overheads, pricing around one third of retail.[^manly]

PLC Sydney’s P&F shop is named **PLC Sydney Preloved Uniforms**. All items are donated. Stock varies. Sale proceeds go to the P&F. Donation tubs sit at reception. The P&F researched similar schools, found they do not offer consignment, and **stopped offering consignment**.[^plc]

Vardys Road Public School P&C’s adopted policy (2018) is the same shape in a public school: the uniform shop stocks second-hand at reduced prices, paid on the day, sold “as is”, parents donate good-quality pieces, lost property that cannot be returned is added at end of term, old sport styles and clearance stock are refused.[^vardys]

Unattended **donation bins** are part of the same model, not a separate product. West Pymble P&C asks families to put resalable uniforms, bags, and accessories in a box in the hallway across from the office; stained or torn pieces go to a WornUp textile-recycling container, not onto the rack.[^wpps] Manly’s official shop page says: drop good-quality used uniforms at the school office; new uniforms are bought from Pickles, not from the P&C rack.[^manly-shop]

Some shops sell **only** preloved and send new-uniform buyers to a preferred supplier. Balwyn High School Chaplaincy’s second-hand shop (canteen, Thursday 3–5 pm) is “the only authorised outlet” for second-hand on campus; new items come from Bob Stewart of Kew. It still runs as a staffed shop with a seller sheet, not as a parent listing site.[^balwyn]

### 3.2 Two intake paths, one shop

Every school policy reviewed uses one or both of these paths. Parents do **not** list items online themselves.

**Donation**

- Parent drops a bag of washed current-uniform items.
- No form, or a short donation note.
- Shop owns the stock. 100% of sale proceeds stay with the P&C / school.
- Unlabelled drop-offs are treated as donations after a short hold (Craigburn: one week).[^craigburn]

**Consignment**

- Parent completes a paper form (family name, student, bank details, item list).
- Shop inspects, prices, tags, and holds the garment for a fixed period (commonly 12 months; Tara: two years).
- On sale, the shop keeps a commission and pays the parent the rest (EFT, credit to school fees, or “donate the proceeds”).
- Unsold items are donated to charity / missions unless the owner collects them in a short window.

Schools that still offer both let the parent tick one box on the same form (Tara, Craigburn, The Lakes College).[^tara][^craigburn][^lakes]

### 3.3 Intake rules (almost identical across schools)

These rules repeat on independent, Catholic, and state-school forms.

| Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Current school uniform only | Craigburn, Ferny Grove, Tara, Mentone, CCGS |
| Washed / pressed; blazers and winter skirts dry-cleaned | Tara, CCGS, Ferny Grove, Mentone |
| Good condition: no stains, holes, fading, broken zips | Ferny Grove (explicit 7-point list), Mentone, CCGS |
| Shop manager has final accept/reject | Ferny Grove, CCGS, The Lakes |
| Reject close-body items: socks, hats/caps, swimwear, bathers, zooties | Tara, CCGS, The Lakes |
| Name labels removed | The Lakes, Mentone (unique ID instead of names) |
| Unfit items are not returned; they go to charity or a free bin | Tara, Mentone, Ratcliffe-style PA shops |

Ferny Grove State High P&C’s acceptance policy is a clean public-school example of the full list.[^ferny]

### 3.4 Pricing

Shops, not parents, set the price after inspection.

| School | Rule |
|---|---|
| Craigburn Primary (SA) | Cannot sell above **50% of listed new price**. Committee sets prices. |
| Mentone Grammar P&F (VIC) | About **50–60% of new retail**. |
| Manly Campus P&C (NSW) | About **one third of retail** (example: $20 jumper vs ~$80 new). |
| The Lakes College (QLD) | Half the price of new. |
| Ratcliffe-style PA shops | About half new price for good condition. |

A parent-facing rule of thumb used in the marketplace world is the same number: anything above 50% of retail is overpriced for second-hand.[^rethread-buy]

**Do not let parents set shop prices.** That is a C2C behaviour. In the shop model the volunteer prices from a list.

### 3.5 Commission on consignment

When consignment exists, the split is a published percentage. It is not negotiated per garment.

| School | Shop keeps | Parent receives | Payout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Craigburn Primary | 50% | 50% credited to school invoices | School fees credit |
| Tara Anglican | 50% | 50% to nominated account | EFT |
| The Lakes College | 50% | 50% | After sale; not paid upfront |
| Ferny Grove SHS P&C | **40%** | 60% | EFT only, no cheques; batched |
| Balwyn High Chaplaincy | **30% + $2** per payment | 70% minus $2 | Direct deposit after Term 2 and Term 4 |
| St Paul’s School (QLD) retail shop | **30%** (flat **$35** on blazers) | remainder | Direct deposit to an Australian bank account |
| Christ Church Grammar (WA) | **25%** | 75% | EFT, school fees, or donate to needy families |
| Mentone Grammar P&F | **25%** | 75% | EFT at end of the term of sale |

Hold period is usually **12 months** (CCGS, Ferny Grove, Mentone). Tara holds **two years**. Unsold stock is donated to charity / missions; CCGS also offers a two-week collect window.[^ccgs][^ferny][^tara][^mentone]

### 3.6 Buyer terms used by shops

School forms commonly say second-hand is sold “as is”, no exchange, no refund, inspect at purchase (Vardys Road, Ferny Grove, Tara, Balwyn — try-on in change rooms).[^vardys][^ferny][^tara][^balwyn]

That wording is **too absolute under Australian Consumer Law** when the P&C is selling in trade or commerce. See §5. The product should copy the *intent* (no change-of-mind on worn garments; defects must be disclosed) without copying an unlawful “no refunds” sign.

### 3.7 Opening hours and volunteer load

These shops are not 24/7 retail. Typical pattern:

- A few mornings a week at drop-off / pickup (Craigburn: Wed/Fri 8:45–9:15 and 2:45–3:30).
- First Wednesday and third Thursday of the month plus orientation days (Manly).
- Term-time one morning a fortnight (PLC Sydney).

The volunteer bottleneck is **intake, tagging, and consignment payouts**, not the till. PLC Sydney dropped consignment for that reason.[^plc] Mentone warns parents that volunteers cannot check quality during shop hours and that processing unfit bags “takes up many hours of volunteer time”.[^mentone]

Digitising the shop should shrink that bottleneck. It should not add a new one (parent photo listings, chat, disputes).

### 3.8 What happens to unsold or non-uniform items

- Recycle or H&M-style textile drop (Manly).
- WornUp yarn recycling for stained/torn branded uniform (West Pymble).[^wpps]
- Charity / missions, or it becomes shop property (CCGS, Tara, Mentone, St Paul’s after 12 months).[^stpauls]
- College care / health centre (The Lakes).
- Lost-property leftovers become donations (Vardys Road).
- Unfit bags discarded without notice (Balwyn: “If you wouldn’t buy it, don’t bring it in for sale”).[^balwyn]

There is no Australian school convention of returning rejected bags to every family. Tara is explicit: items deemed unfit are not returned.[^tara]

## 4. Parent-to-parent classifieds (do not copy into the shop)

This is the **most used overflow**, not the shop’s job.

- Official school Facebook page comments.
- Unofficial school parent Facebook groups.
- Suburb community groups and Facebook Marketplace.
- Gumtree.

Schools themselves still point families there. Ocean Reef Senior High’s official news (29 Oct 2025) tells families to join a school Facebook group “to buy, sell, or exchange second-hand school uniforms and textbooks”.[^oceanreef]

Nine.com.au treats these as the next stop after the school office.[^nine] Rethread describes the operational failure mode P&Cs already know: no central catalogue, cash handling, stale posts, and no record of who is owed what.[^rethread-pc]

Fulfilment is “meet at the gate, PayID or cash”. Consumer guarantees generally **do not** apply to one-off private sales (title / undisturbed possession / no hidden securities still do).[^accc-guarantees][^nsw-private]

UniformOrder should not replace Facebook groups. A P&C that wants parent-to-parent listing already has Rethread, UniformHub, KidKit, Second Best, The Uniform Exchange, and Sustainable School Shop.

## 5. Dedicated C2C platforms (adjacent, not the shop)

These products exist. They solve a different job: **parent lists a photo, another parent pays, they arrange handover**.

| Platform | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [The Uniform Exchange](https://www.theuniformexchange.com.au/) | Free personal listings + a physical Pymble shop that sells on behalf of selected North Shore schools | Long-running; FAQ tells members to meet in a public place.[^tue-faq] |
| [Rethread](https://rethread.com.au/) | Free listings, every Australian school pre-created | Pitches P&Cs two modes: families list to each other, **or** the P&C lists donated stock under a P&C account.[^rethread-pc] |
| [KidKit](https://www.kidkitapp.com/) | Parent listings, Stripe hold until received, **10% seller fee** | Quality grades: New / Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair.[^kidkit] |
| [Second Best](https://secondbest.com.au/) | Parent listings, Stripe hold until received | Marketplace, not a school shop. |
| [UniformHub](https://uniformhub.com.au/) | School-circle listings; “meet at pickup”; PayID / cash / claimed escrow | C2C positioning (“faster than Facebook”). |
| [Sustainable School Shop](https://www.sustainableschoolshop.com.au/) | School-signed-up classifieds for uniforms and textbooks | Buyer contacts seller and arranges the transaction. |

Rethread is honest that some P&Cs “keep it simple and let families list and sell directly to each other” while others “collect donated uniforms and list them centrally under a P&C account”.[^rethread-pc] Only the second of those is a shop workflow. The first is Facebook with better search.

**Do not build a fourth C2C marketplace.** The category is crowded, the trust model is different (private sale vs shop as seller of record), and it fights UniformOrder’s existing Stripe Connect + school-as-seller design. An independent research pass listed “parent-to-parent listings plus held-funds checkout” as something shop software might copy. That is a different product. UniformOrder should copy the **shop** channels (donation bin, donated rack, optional consignment). Leave Facebook and Rethread to do Facebook and Rethread.

## 6. Australian Consumer Law

### 6.1 Shop sales (P&C / school is the seller)

When the uniform shop sells a preloved garment, it is selling **in trade or commerce**. Consumer guarantees apply to second-hand goods, taking age, price, and condition into account.[^accc-guarantees][^nsw-secondhand]

Buyers can expect the garment to be of acceptable quality *for a used item at that price*, to match its description, and to be fit for the ordinary purpose (wear as school uniform). Hidden defects must be disclosed in plain language. Calling something “as is” or “seconds” is **not enough**.[^nsw-secondhand]

ACCC product-safety guidance (June 2025): consumer guarantees apply to all second-hand sales **except** private sales between two people where the seller is not running a business. Listings must be complete and accurate. Keep a customer record in case of a safety issue.[^accc-safety]

A P&C cannot lawfully display a blanket “no refunds on second-hand”. Change-of-mind can still be refused. Faults that a reasonable buyer would not have accepted, given the disclosed condition, still need a remedy.

### 6.2 Private parent-to-parent sales

Most consumer guarantees do **not** apply. Title, undisturbed possession, and no undisclosed securities still do.[^accc-guarantees][^nsw-private]

That is another reason not to mix C2C listings into the school shop: the legal role of the seller changes, and UniformOrder’s current legal model is “the school is the seller of record”.

### 6.3 What the product should disclose

Copy the shop practice, in ACL-safe words:

- Condition band (for example Good / Fair) and any known defect (mark, repair, faded crest).
- “Preloved — sold as worn. Change of mind is not offered. Australian Consumer Law still applies if the item is not as described or not of acceptable quality for a used garment.”
- Inspect at collection where possible.

## 7. GST

### 7.1 New uniforms

If the P&C is registered for GST, **new** uniform sales are taxable. Education course fees are GST-free; uniforms are not. ATO Community support pointed P&C treasurers at GSTR 2000/30.[^ato-community-uniforms]

Canberra High School P&C’s GST manual treats the uniform shop as a **commercial activity**, not a fundraising event: GST at 10% on all new items; GST credits on supplier invoices.[^chs-gst]

### 7.2 Donated second-hand goods

GST Act s 38-255: a supply of second-hand goods is GST-free if:

1. the supplier is an **endorsed charity**, a **gift-deductible entity**, or a **government school**, and
2. the goods were received as a **gift**, or as a GST-free second-hand supply from another such entity,

unless the supplier has changed the original character of the goods. Cleaning or repairing does not change character.[^gst-act][^ato-donated]

CHS P&C’s manual applies this in practice: “Donated second hand goods are sold as GST free ie 0% GST in the price.”[^chs-gst]

ATO: GST-free donated second-hand sales still count toward GST turnover.[^ato-donated]

### 7.3 Consignment is not a gift

s 38-255 requires the goods to have been **gifted**. A consignment is the parent’s property held for sale. Those sales are generally **not** GST-free under s 38-255. A GST-registered shop should treat consignment resale as taxable unless their accountant has a written basis not to.

This is a hard product split: **donation lines can be GST-free (if the tenant qualifies); consignment lines usually cannot.**

St Paul’s consignment form has the parent certify they have not provided an ABN because “the transaction is wholly of a private or domestic nature from my perspective.”[^stpauls] That is the parent’s tax position, not the shop’s. The shop is still the retailer.

s 38-250 (GST-free non-commercial supplies by eligible charities when consideration is under 50% of GST-inclusive market value) was **not** seen applied to any inspected uniform price list. Do not use it as a product default.

### 7.4 Not every P&C qualifies

s 38-255 is limited to endorsed charities, certain GDEs, and government schools. Many P&Cs are incorporated associations. Some are endorsed; some are not. The product must not silently assume GST-free. Each tenant (or their accountant) must say whether donated preloved is GST-free for them.

Current UniformOrder totals always take **1/11 of the GST-inclusive total** (`apps/web/src/lib/order-totals.ts`). A mixed cart of new (taxable) and donated preloved (GST-free) needs a line-level GST flag. That is new work. See the plan.

## 8. What “trading” means in this market

Parents say “buy, sell, swap, donate”. In school practice those words map to:

| Everyday word | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Donate** | Drop a bag at the shop. Shop sells. P&C keeps the money. |
| **Sell** (through the shop) | Consignment form + 12-month hold + commission + EFT. |
| **Sell** (privately) | Facebook / Marketplace / Rethread. Meet at the gate. |
| **Swap / trade** | Informal parent-to-parent. Not a shop process. Nine.com.au mentions asking if the school will “purchase items you no longer need or perhaps swap” — that is still the office/shop, not a barter engine.[^nine] |
| **Buy** | Pay the shop (or the other parent) and collect. |

There is **no widely used school barter ledger** (credits, token swaps, garment-for-garment matching). Do not invent one.

## 9. What UniformOrder should copy vs ignore

### Copy (school-shop convention)

1. Preloved lives **inside the existing uniform shop**, same seller of record, same pickup.
2. Parents **drop off**; volunteers **list**. Parents do not self-publish listings.
3. Default path is **donation**. Consignment is optional and heavier.
4. Shop sets price from a list, typically **≤ 50% of new**, often ~⅓.
5. Accept only **current, clean, repairable** uniform. Refuse socks, swimwear, hats in many schools.
6. Unique ticket / family record only when consigning.
7. Unsold after 12 months → charity.
8. Payout options: EFT, school-fee credit, or donate proceeds.
9. “Buy before you buy new” in the parent catalogue.
10. GST-free only for **donated** stock, and only if the tenant qualifies.

### Do not copy / do not invent

- Parent photo listings and in-app chat.
- Escrow released at the school gate.
- Nationwide search across tenants.
- Swap credits or garment-for-garment matching.
- Parent-set prices on shop stock.
- Blanket “no refunds” that fights ACL.
- Assuming every P&C gets s 38-255 GST-free treatment.
- Competing with Rethread / KidKit / Facebook as a C2C network.

## 10. Uncertainties (do not treat as fact)

- How many UniformOrder target schools still run consignment vs donation-only. PLC Sydney’s research of “several similar schools” found donation-only was the peer norm, but independent schools in the sample still run 25–50% consignment.
- Whether a given P&C is an endorsed charity / GDE / government school for s 38-255. Accountant question per tenant. P&Cs are not government schools by default.
- Whether s 38-250 (under 50% of market value) could also make some donated sales GST-free for eligible entities. Not observed on shop price lists. Do not encode it.
- Whether school-fee credit as consignment payout is allowed at state schools (Craigburn does it; independent schools often offer it; state-school finance rules vary).
- Hygiene rules for hats: Tara refuses hats; some primary shops still sell hats. Make the refuse-list a tenant setting, defaulting to the common set (socks, swimwear, hats).
- Second-hand clothing is not generally a mandatory-standard category in the way cots or nightwear can be. ACCC still requires safety and honest description. Flag any school-specific branding or reflective-safety items if they appear.
